# Notation and Constitutional Primitives

The minimum kernel is intentionally small.

## Observation and admission

`O` denotes observed reality. It may be partial, stale, contradictory, or malformed.

`O*` denotes admitted observation: aligned, grounded, bounded, and associated with the exact subject under consideration.

\[
O \not\Rightarrow O^*
\]

Observation is not admission.

## Manufacture

`μ` is a partial lawful manufacturing function. It does not mean arbitrary generation.

\[
\mu : O^* \rightharpoonup A \times R_a
\]

A manufacture may refuse. A candidate artifact can exist without operational authority.

## Artifact and actuation

`A` denotes a manufactured artifact or consequential state transition. Context determines whether it is purely constructed or has crossed into DO.

SELECT chooses among possibilities. CONSTRUCT manufactures reversible candidates. DO causes consequential mutation.

\[
SELECT \neq CONSTRUCT \neq DO
\]

## Receipt

`R` denotes evidence that binds the exact subject, operation, authority, environment, toolchain, consequence, postcondition, and replay identity.

A named object called a receipt is not automatically a constitutional receipt. Its fields and verification behavior must establish the required relationships.

## Standing

The repository constitution recognizes explicit standing vocabulary. This handbook uses `UNKNOWN`, `OBSERVED`, `CANDIDATE`, `PARTIAL_ALIVE`, `ALIVE`, `BLOCKED`, `UNSUPPORTED`, `REJECTED`, and `SUPERSEDED` where applicable, and uses typed refusal to distinguish unlawful or malformed transitions from unavailable capability.

`ALIVE` requires observed execution and evidence against the exact admitted subject. Compilation, inspection, workflow existence, mock-only behavior, or an HTTP 200 are insufficient by themselves.

## Closure

A closure is not a claim that the world is complete. It is a bounded claim that a particular obligation has been satisfied for a particular subject under explicit falsifiers.

The book distinguishes epistemic, representational, constructive, formal, experimental, operational, evidentiary, replay, interface, infrastructure, software, economic, security, class, and civilization closure.
