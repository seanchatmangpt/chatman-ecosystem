## Research claim

**Final Falsifier** is treated here as a law-state problem rather than a slogan. The chapter asks which observations are admissible, which transformations are lawful, what authority is required for consequence, which receipts establish standing, and what replay or transfer would be required to keep that standing over time. The Chateco position is deliberately bounded: adjacency is not equivalence, execution is not proof, and absence of evidence does not become refusal by narration.

## Preserved source development

A strong universal claim requires a strong refutation discipline. A proposed counterexample must preserve the target domain's objects, morphisms, admission conditions, closure, actuation, recognition, receipt or replay, boundaries, and exclusions. Otherwise it may be adjacent to the thesis without contradicting it.

Four recurring apparent counterexamples were identified.

First, admission is hidden inside the transformation. A system says it applies a function directly to raw input, but the function contains parsing, validation, filtering, or assumptions that determine which input is usable. The boundary exists even if unnamed.

Second, observation is hidden inside the artifact. A supposedly spontaneous result depends on prior state encoded in weights, memory, environment, institutions, or inherited structure.

Third, transformation is hidden inside words such as emergence. Naming a discontinuity does not show that no relation connects prior state to consequence.

Fourth, receipt is removed. A physical event may occur without evidence or recognized standing. This does not refute the receipt requirement; it produces an event whose authoritative status cannot be distinguished from assertion.

A genuine falsifier must therefore demonstrate recognized standing while none of these structures is recoverable. The thesis retains that condition as an open challenge and records attempted counterexamples in Appendix O.

## Exclusions

This chapter does **not** infer universal truth from one implementation, formal correctness from compilation, production authority from credentials, successful execution from workflow existence, or class closure from one solved instance. It does not treat a public ontology as automatically legitimate, a proof kernel as a sensor of external reality, or a receipt-shaped record as sufficient when exact identity and consequence are missing.

## Falsifier

A refutation must preserve the same native objects and recognition boundary. The chapter is falsified if a competing system can obtain the same recognized standing while no equivalent admission boundary, lawful transformation, authority condition, consequential actuation boundary, or receipt/replay relation can be recovered. A merely adjacent mechanism, renamed vocabulary, or different definition of success is evidence of another design, not yet a refutation.
