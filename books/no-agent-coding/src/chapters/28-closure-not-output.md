# 28. Closure, Not Output

**Executive thesis:** The productivity metric for an AI-saturated engineering organization should move from artifacts produced to semantic classes closed.

## Why output becomes cheap

Lines of code, commits, pull requests, tests, documents, and agent actions can all increase without reducing uncertainty. When production is abundant, output metrics reward inventory. They encourage organizations to celebrate candidates before they become integrated, replayable capabilities.

## Closure is different

A class is closer to closed when its semantics, authority, construction, verification, consequence, receipts, and replay are explicit enough that future known instances do not require rediscovery. Closure can reduce future activity, which makes it invisible to metrics that reward motion.

## Little’s Law again

Reducing future arrivals is often more valuable than increasing service rate. If a ggen pack or semantic contract removes hundreds of recurring interpretation steps, the visible engineering queue shrinks. That is a success even if commit count declines.

## Operating practice

Track rediscovery rate, admission lead time, admitted WIP, deterministic projection coverage, replay success, and agent-retirement rate. Use output metrics only as diagnostics, never as the primary definition of enterprise value.

## Diagnostic question

What activity metric would fall if you successfully eliminated a recurring class of work?
