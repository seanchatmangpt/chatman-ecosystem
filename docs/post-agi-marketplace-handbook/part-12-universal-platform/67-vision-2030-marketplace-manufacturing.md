# 67. Vision 2030: Marketplace Manufacturing

> **Standing:** this chapter is a target architecture. It does not claim that the 2030 capabilities described here exist today.

## Software markets become compilation targets

The mature endpoint of marketplace engineering is not a larger integration team. It is a manufacturing system in which a new commercial market is primarily a new semantic and qualification target.

Today, teams read documentation, copy examples, create marketplace-specific product records, implement entitlement handlers, hand-enter pricing, assemble security evidence, and rediscover the same lifecycle defects. By 2030, the reusable portion of that work should be represented as executable commercial knowledge.

```text
Observed market contract
  → O
  → admission
  → O*
  → canonical mappings + constraints
  → marketplace pack
  → ggen projections
  → formal / gym qualification
  → sandbox / live execution
  → receipt
  → standing
```

The result is not a universal API that pretends all marketplaces are identical. It is a universal manufacturing calculus that preserves differences explicitly.

## Procurement becomes protocolized

Enterprise procurement contains recurring objects: organization identity, product identity, seller identity, security evidence, approved terms, offers, agreements, entitlements, deployment constraints, support commitments, and financial routing.

A large portion of the coordination around those objects can become machine-readable and replayable. Security questionnaires become projections of a control-evidence graph. Vendor onboarding forms become projections of admitted seller facts. Private-offer construction becomes a constrained transformation of canonical plans and buyer-scoped deltas.

The human/legal/business authorities do not disappear. Their decisions become explicit inputs rather than invisible pauses in an email thread.

## Contracts become executable constraints

An approved agreement can project selected operational obligations into policy:

```text
approved residency term → allowed deployment regions
approved support tier → support/SLO policy
approved quantity → entitlement quantity
approved term → effective interval
approved retention rule → data lifecycle policy
```

The system never infers legal meaning from ambiguous prose and then actuates it. The translation itself is admitted and versioned.

## Entitlement becomes the universal rights boundary

Every route to market—direct, cloud marketplace, enterprise application store, data marketplace, reseller, sovereign market—eventually needs to answer what the customer is currently allowed to receive.

A canonical entitlement graph allows runtime authorization and fulfillment to remain stable while purchase protocols change. Vendor events are normalized observations; they do not become parallel sources of product rights.

## Continuous requalification

Vendor contracts, APIs, security programs, package validators, pricing primitives, and partner rules evolve. A 2030 platform should continuously detect source drift, regenerate affected projections, execute the cheapest qualification ladder, and surface only the capabilities whose standing changed.

```text
vendor source drift
  → impacted semantic edges
  → regenerate dependency closure
  → requalify
  → compare receipts
  → update standing
```

This replaces annual integration archaeology with bounded machine-time maintenance.

## Bounded autonomous operation

Machine intelligence can diagnose a rejected meter, construct a corrected projection, verify an entitlement discrepancy, or prepare a renewed private offer at machine speed. Consequential action remains BRCE-controlled.

The target is not “AI can do anything.” The target is a system in which every lawful recurrent commercial transition can be executed quickly because identity, policy, authority, and evidence are already typed.

## What must remain external

Some facts should remain stubbornly boring:

- a marketplace approves or rejects seller admission;
- a buyer accepts or rejects an offer;
- legal authority approves terms;
- finance/accounting authority owns accounting policy;
- customer security/risk teams own their risk acceptance;
- credentials do not manufacture permission;
- successful generation does not manufacture vendor approval.

The post-AGI gain comes from eliminating avoidable coordination around these authorities, not pretending they do not exist.
