## Research claim

**Cybersecurity Review** is treated here as a law-state problem rather than a slogan. The chapter asks which observations are admissible, which transformations are lawful, what authority is required for consequence, which receipts establish standing, and what replay or transfer would be required to keep that standing over time. The Chateco position is deliberately bounded: adjacency is not equivalence, execution is not proof, and absence of evidence does not become refusal by narration.

## Preserved source development

The Bounded Receipted Chatman Equation operationalizes the thesis at the mutation boundary. Its hard invariant is:

\\\[ \\boxed{\\text{zero unreceipted actuation}}. \\\]

A diagnosis may manufacture an intent. A planner may manufacture a plan. A proof may establish that the plan preserves a property. None of these objects is permission to change machine or physical state. Authorization is a separate transformation that binds principal, scope, capability, plan identity, boundary, and validity conditions.

The broker is the only DO path. It accepts an authorized envelope, selects a deterministic adapter, performs the bounded operation, and returns either a typed failure or an artifact awaiting receipt. Hooks cannot actuate. N3 rules cannot actuate. Planners cannot actuate. Language models cannot actuate. Proof kernels cannot actuate. They may contribute evidence or intents to the path, but mutation authority remains localized.

This separation creates a replayable chain:

```text
observation -> admitted condition -> plan -> authorization envelope
            -> broker dispatch -> adapter result -> receipt -> replay
```

The decisive acceptance test exercises the entire cycle. Repository mechanics are observed. A missing witness becomes a planning goal. A planner selects a repair operator. The result is projected into POWL. The broker issues an authorization envelope. A deterministic adapter applies the structural patch. The resulting bytes and witness are receipted. Success is not the presence of a plausible diff; it is closure across the full chain.

## Exclusions

This chapter does **not** infer universal truth from one implementation, formal correctness from compilation, production authority from credentials, successful execution from workflow existence, or class closure from one solved instance. It does not treat a public ontology as automatically legitimate, a proof kernel as a sensor of external reality, or a receipt-shaped record as sufficient when exact identity and consequence are missing.
