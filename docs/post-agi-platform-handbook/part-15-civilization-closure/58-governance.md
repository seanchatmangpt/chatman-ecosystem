# 58. Post-AGI Governance

Governance for highly capable intelligence should avoid an impossible objective: preventing the intelligence from thinking dangerous thoughts.

The architecture instead governs transitions from thought to consequence.

## Bound consequence, not imagination

A post-AGI system can be allowed to explore broad counterfactual spaces inside CONSTRUCT.

The governing principle is:

\[
Maximize(CONSTRUCT_{lawful})
\]

while:

\[
Minimize(AmbientDO)
\]

This lets intelligence remain creative and adaptive without making every internal candidate a potential external action.

## Governance as calculus

Policies should participate in admission and authority rather than live only as prose.

ODRL, SHACL, formal constraints, capability boundaries, and typed refusals can encode parts of governance mechanically. Human or institutional authority can remain explicit where values cannot be reduced to a deterministic rule.

## No self-certification

A system may propose changes to its own ontology, policy, manufacturing rules, or validators. Those proposals are CONSTRUCT.

They do not become authoritative merely because the system predicts the changes are improvements.

Constitutional self-modification requires a higher-order admission path whose authority is itself explicit.

## Governance needs falsifiers

A policy that can never be shown wrong becomes dogma. A governance claim should state what evidence would cause review, supersession, or refusal.

This is especially important when machine systems optimize against measurable rules.

## Receipted consequence

Governance becomes inspectable when consequential transitions carry the policy and authority context under which they were admitted.

Auditors can then evaluate what rule actually governed the action rather than reconstructing intent from logs and organizational charts.

## Falsifier

A governance system is not bounded if an intelligence can modify the rule that grants its authority and then immediately use the expanded authority without an independent admission boundary.
