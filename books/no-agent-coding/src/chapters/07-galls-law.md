# 7. Gall’s Law and Working Piers

**Executive thesis:** A complex deterministic enterprise has to accrete from smaller systems that actually work; plausible completeness is not standing.

## The anti-big-bang rule

A large architecture diagram can be coherent while none of its operational claims have been earned. Agent swarms make big-bang construction even more seductive because many files and services can appear quickly. Gall’s Law forces a different cadence: each layer depends on a predecessor that already has demonstrated standing.

## A Gall checkpoint

A useful checkpoint leaves behind a bounded working system, a positive witness, a negative falsifier, an exact subject identity, a deterministic receipt, and replay outside the originating session. A generated artifact cannot prove its own generator, and a green build cannot by itself prove the claimed runtime consequence.

## Constitution first, activation second

Conway-oriented architecture may declare the whole semantic topology early: capabilities, relations, authorities, and projection families. Gall does not require ignorance of the future. It requires operational complexity to be earned incrementally. Constitutional topology is not the same thing as activated operational complexity.

## Operating practice

Choose the smallest capability that crosses a real boundary and can produce an independently observable consequence. Make it work, preserve it, encode its falsifier, then extend. Resist milestones that rename partial evidence as completion merely to maintain schedule optics.

## Diagnostic question

What is the smallest independently working pier beneath your largest architecture claim?
