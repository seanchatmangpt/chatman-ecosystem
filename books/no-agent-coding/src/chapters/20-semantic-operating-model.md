# 20. The Semantic Operating Model

**Executive thesis:** Enterprise architecture should begin with how the company chooses to integrate and standardize meaning, authority, evidence, and manufacture—not merely applications and infrastructure.

## Operating model before technology

An enterprise that wants local autonomy can tolerate more local semantic choice. An enterprise that depends on global customer identity, entitlements, security policy, cost controls, or cross-cloud operations needs stronger shared semantics. The operating model determines which concepts must be common and which can remain local.

## Four forms of standardization

No Agent Coding adds semantic, authority, manufacturing, and evidence standardization to familiar process and data concerns. Semantic standardization says what shared concepts mean. Authority standardization says who may cause consequences. Manufacturing standardization says how representations are derived. Evidence standardization says what qualifies a claim.

## Avoid universal centralization

A canonical graph is not an excuse to centralize every decision. DfCM favors bounded local projections where variation is lawful. The enterprise standardizes only the invariants needed for interoperability, governance, reuse, and standing. Everything else can remain a local extension.

## Operating practice

For each core capability, decide explicitly: what must be globally identical, what must interoperate, what may vary, and what evidence crosses boundaries. Encode those decisions in the semantic operating model before choosing another platform layer.

## Diagnostic question

Which semantics, authorities, manufacturing rules, and evidence rules truly need enterprise standardization?
