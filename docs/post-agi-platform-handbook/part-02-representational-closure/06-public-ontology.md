# 6. Public Ontology as Civilization Memory

A post-AGI intelligence should not invent a private vocabulary every time it encounters a concept humanity has already modeled.

Public ontology is therefore not decorative metadata. It is an accumulation mechanism for civilization memory.

## Reuse meaning before inventing meaning

The ecosystem prefers established semantic vocabularies where they fit:

- **PROV-O** for entities, activities, agents, and provenance;
- **DCAT** for catalogs and datasets;
- **DCTERMS** for durable resource metadata;
- **SKOS** for concepts, schemes, broader/narrower relationships, and controlled vocabularies;
- **SHACL** for graph constraints and validation;
- **ODRL** for permissions, prohibitions, duties, and policy expressions;
- **FOAF** where established agent/person relationships are sufficient;
- **QUDT** for quantities, units, dimensions, and numeric semantics;
- **SOSA/SSN** for observations, sensors, actuators, samples, and features of interest;
- **FIBO** where financial semantics are genuinely required;
- **OCEL 2.0** for object-centric event histories and process state.

The objective is not to maximize acronyms. It is to minimize unnecessary semantic invention.

## Custom ontology remains necessary

Public ontology cannot encode every novel constitutional distinction. Concepts such as an ecosystem-specific standing lattice, BRCE transition, a particular class-closure certificate, or a ggen pack may require custom terms.

The rule is additive:

\[
O_{total} = O_{public} \cup O_{custom}
\]

with custom terms linked to public semantics wherever lawful.

This keeps novel concepts explicit without forking common concepts into an isolated dialect.

## Civilization memory requires executable correspondence

An ontology becomes more valuable when it connects to construction and evidence.

A concept that can be queried, constrained, rendered by ggen, admitted formally, actuated through BRCE, receipted, and replayed is not merely descriptive knowledge. It is reusable operational knowledge.

That gives a path from discovery to inheritance:

\[
Discovery \rightarrow Concept \rightarrow Ontology \rightarrow Construction \rightarrow Evidence \rightarrow Class\ Memory
\]

The next intelligence does not need to rediscover the class from prose.

## Semantic portability beats framework loyalty

Framework-specific schemas age quickly. Public semantic relationships often survive many generations of tooling.

A capability described as an `odrl:Policy` governing a `prov:Activity` with a QUDT-measured resource constraint can be projected into systems that do not yet exist today.

That is important in a post-AGI architecture: the semantic half-life should exceed the implementation half-life.

## Falsifier

If the organization creates a custom term that is semantically equivalent to a stable public term without a demonstrated boundary difference, the ontology has introduced representational WIP.

## Operational exercise

Audit one platform schema. For each local field, ask whether its meaning already exists in a public ontology. Preserve local terms only when they add real domain semantics or necessary constitutional distinctions.
