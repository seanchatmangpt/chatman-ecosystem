# 10. DfCM: Design for Combinatorial Maximalism

Human architecture often optimizes by reducing choices. Standardize on one database. Publish one golden path. Approve one cloud pattern. Constrain developers to the few configurations a platform team can support.

Those strategies are rational under scarce human cognition and expensive coordination.

Post-AGI intelligence changes the constraint.

DfCM—Design for Combinatorial Maximalism—asks the system to preserve the maximum set of **reversible, lawful** constructions until evidence justifies irreversible selection.

## The admissible possibility graph

Let `C` be the set of constructible candidate states and `E` the lawful transformations among them.

\[
G_C=(C,E)
\]

The goal is not to maximize arbitrary possibility. The graph is bounded by ontology, capability, authority class, cost, evidence, safety, and the exact subject.

Within those bounds, diversity is information.

## One failed edge is topology

Traditional automation often encodes one procedural path. When step seven fails, the workflow fails.

DfCM treats failure differently. If one edge is unavailable, that is evidence about the graph.

\[
e_i \notin E_{admissible}
\]

does not imply:

\[
G_C = \varnothing
\]

The intelligence can route around the failed edge if another lawful path exists.

This is the post-AGI analogue of adaptation in game-playing systems, except infrastructure and process domains can often provide far richer state information than imperfect-information games.

## Preserve reversibility

A candidate configuration, plan, proof, simulation, generated API, or synthetic OCEL history can be constructed without changing reality. Those operations should be abundant.

Irreversible selection should occur late.

This gives a general principle:

\[
|CONSTRUCT| \gg |DO|
\]

The system may explore millions of candidate worlds and actuate one.

## Golden paths become historical priors

A golden path does not disappear. It becomes a high-standing region of the construction graph: a path with historical evidence, known costs, known failure behavior, and reusable receipts.

That makes golden paths more useful, not less. They are priors rather than prisons.

An intelligence can choose the known path when it dominates the objective function, or construct alternatives when the admitted constraints make the prior path invalid.

## Constraint intersection

Candidate selection can be expressed as intersection:

\[
C^* = C_{ontology} \cap C_{policy} \cap C_{cost} \cap C_{security} \cap C_{runtime} \cap C_{authority}
\]

Formal admission and empirical GymAct execution can shrink the set further before DO.

## Falsifier

A system is not practicing DfCM if it destroys reversible alternatives before a relevant constraint requires selection, or if it uses “maximalism” to include unlawful or unauthorized candidates.

## Operational exercise

Take a current golden path and model it as one route through a capability graph. Add at least two lawful alternative routes. Record which constraints remove each route. The result should explain *why* a path is selected rather than merely naming it standard.
