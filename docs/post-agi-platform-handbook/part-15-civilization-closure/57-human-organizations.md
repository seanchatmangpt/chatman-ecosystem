# 57. Human Organizations as Legacy Execution Engines

Organizations are not merely groups of people. They are execution systems built from roles, queues, documents, meetings, approvals, incentives, memories, and authority relationships.

In that sense, a human organization is a legacy process engine.

## Queues encode scarce cognition

Tickets, inboxes, backlogs, review queues, and change boards exist partly because human attention is finite. They serialize work so limited operators can manage it.

Post-AGI systems should preserve the authority and policy functions of those queues while questioning whether the waiting itself remains necessary.

## Coordination is often representation repair

Many meetings exist because several teams maintain separate representations of the same underlying capability.

One team owns Terraform, another CI, another policy, another portal, another docs, another runbook. Coordination synchronizes those copies.

Canonical semantic manufacture can eliminate the synchronization work instead of merely scheduling the meeting more efficiently.

## Institutional memory is lossy

Human organizations store critical constraints in stories: “we tried that in 2022,” “security requires this,” “finance won't allow that region,” “don't touch this script.”

AutoFDE and ggen-legacy should recover these constraints into explicit ontology, evidence, and falsifiers before the humans who remember them disappear.

## Roles become authority profiles

The valuable part of an organizational role is often not the job title but the authority and obligations attached to it.

A post-AGI system can model those relationships directly while still allowing humans to occupy the role where law, policy, or preference requires it.

## People are not deprecated

Calling the organization a legacy engine does not imply that humans are defects. It identifies the historical execution mechanism so its functions can be preserved deliberately rather than romanticized or erased.

Humans may remain sources of goals, values, consent, judgment, creativity, local knowledge, or authority even when routine coordination disappears.

## Falsifier

A reengineered organization has not eliminated coordination work if the same semantic disagreement still exists and an agent merely attends the meeting on behalf of the human.

## Operational exercise

Choose one recurring cross-team meeting. Identify which agenda items are semantic synchronization, evidence review, authority decision, goal negotiation, or genuinely novel reasoning. Only the first category is an obvious candidate for elimination through shared projections.
