# 47. Capability Markets

Once capabilities have semantic identities, evidence, cost, latency, locality, and authority requirements, an intelligence can select among providers without relying on brand names or manually curated integration tables.

This creates the basis for a capability market.

## Capability profile

A provider profile can be modeled as:

\[
C_i=(O_i, I_i, P_i, E_i, L_i, Cost_i, A_i)
\]

where the dimensions represent ontology coverage, interfaces, policy constraints, evidence standing, latency, economic cost, and authority requirements.

The exact tuple can vary by domain.

## Markets are semantic matching problems

A consumer declares an admitted need. The system searches for providers whose capability graph satisfies that need.

Selection can include local implementations, cloud services, open-source tools, specialized agents, or other autonomous systems.

The market is not restricted to commercial transactions.

## Evidence quality is part of price

A cheap capability with weak evidence may be more expensive operationally than a higher-priced capability with strong historical standing and deterministic replay.

Post-AGI selection can make that trade explicit.

## Delegation and receipts

When a capability is purchased or delegated, the returned artifact should carry the receipts required by the consumer's admission policy.

This allows machine-to-machine commerce without turning trust into an opaque reputation score.

## Marketplaces as public memory

The ggen marketplace can store executable knowledge classes: ontology, generation law, validation packs, gym specifications, and receipt requirements.

A marketplace entry is therefore more than downloadable code. It is a candidate bundle for inheriting a solved class.

## Falsifier

A capability market is semantically weak if provider selection depends on names and descriptions while the system cannot mechanically compare admitted input/output, authority, and evidence obligations.

## Operational exercise

Define one capability request without naming a vendor. Create three provider profiles and rank them under two different admitted objective functions. The ranking should change when the constraints change.
